Strategy validation — Phase 8

Does the strategy have an edge? The evidence says no.

A frozen, unmodified TrendMomentumBaseline was run — unchanged — across 12 sector-diverse instruments, three chronological sub-periods, four cost regimes, three risk levels, and a 3×3 SMA-parameter neighborhood, using only real historical data. Zero strategy, risk, or indicator code was touched. The pooled result is a net loss that gets worse under realistic costs, and every symbol that returned money would have returned dramatically more from simply buying and holding it.

12 symbols, 218 pooled trades 247 tests, 246 passing (1 pre-existing, unrelated) 0 strategy/risk parameters changed 1 real defect found (in the validation script, not the platform) Classification: C
Bottom line

Classification C — no evidence of a durable edge. Pooled across the defined 12-symbol universe: net PnL is −7,547.20 on 218 trades at baseline cost assumptions; the gross (zero-cost) result is barely breakeven (+242.38, ~$1/trade); only 4 of 12 symbols were individually profitable; and every symbol where buy-and-hold was itself profitable beat the strategy's return by a wide margin — in several cases by 100+ percentage points over the same 5 years. The 95% confidence interval on mean R-multiple (−0.385R to +0.041R) straddles zero.

One coherent, non-random-looking pattern emerged from the regime breakdown — trades taken during confirmed strong-uptrend quarters were profitable (+0.30R expectancy, 55% win rate) while trades taken during sideways quarters (68% of all quarters observed) were not (−0.30R, 29% win rate). This is recorded as a specific hypothesis for a future, separately-validated phase — not acted on here, and not sufficient on its own to change the verdict on the strategy as currently gated.

1Frozen strategy specification

Read directly from strategy/baseline.py, market/indicators.py, backtesting/costs.py, and risk/config.py — nothing here was inferred or approximated, and nothing here was changed during this phase.

Strategy: TrendMomentumBaseline v1.0 (strategy/baseline.py)
  Entry (LONG only; ALL must hold on the generating bar):
    trend:    SMA20 > SMA50
    momentum: RSI14 > 50  AND  MACD > MACD signal
    volume:   volume_trend == "increasing" (5-bar normalized slope > +5%)
  Stop:    entry_price - (ATR14 * 1.5)
  Target:  entry_price + (ATR14 * 1.5 * 2.0)      [fixed 2.0 risk:reward]
  Fill:    next bar's open, after slippage; conservative same-bar stop-wins rule

Indicators (market/indicators.py): SMA(20,50), RSI(14, Wilder EWM), MACD(12,26,9),
  ATR(14), volume trend (5-bar linear-slope classification) -- all rolling/ewm,
  causal by construction (see sec 14/17 for the real-data confirmation).

Cost model (default, backtesting/costs.py): brokerage 20 (flat, per fill) +
  5bps entry slippage + 5bps exit slippage. No fees_pct/taxes_pct by default.

Risk (default, risk/config.py): 0.5% equity risked per trade, 3% max daily
  loss, 10% max drawdown (hard halt), 25% max exposure, 3-loss soft
  threshold (50% size reduction), 6-loss hard reject, min R:R 1.5.

Position sizing: floor(equity * risk_pct / (entry - stop)), capped by
  available cash and max_exposure_pct. Single position per symbol.

This specification is the one and only configuration used for every "baseline" number in this report — variants (cost/risk/SMA-neighborhood) are explicitly labeled as variants, never silently substituted for baseline.

A recorded hypothesis, not an action taken

Performance is concentrated in strong-uptrend, low-volatility quarters — coherent for a trend+momentum strategy, and not what a purely random split would look like. But sideways quarters are 68% of ALL quarters observed, and that regime is clearly negative. This is recorded as a hypothesis worth a SEPARATELY validated future phase (e.g., gating entries on a broader trend filter) — per spec §16's explicit data-snooping protection, the baseline was NOT modified and re-run to chase this observation within this phase.

Methodology caveat, stated plainly: each chunk is re-backtested as an independent fresh account (not a continuation of one continuous equity curve), so a position open near a chunk boundary is force-closed there rather than carrying into the next quarter as it would in the continuous full-period run — this is why pooled chunk-level trades (320 across trend labels) exceed the continuous baseline's 218. The regime breakdown is a valid qualitative signal, not a claim that these numbers sum exactly to the Section 3 baseline.

This is not the classic overfitting signature (strong in development, collapsing out-of-sample) — development was actually the WORST of the three sub-periods. The result is consistently weak across all three chronological windows rather than decaying from an initially strong one. The top 5 winning trades account for only 9.2% of total gross profit — the (limited) upside is broadly distributed across many winners, not dependent on one or two lucky trades. That specific concern (spec §10's "does the strategy depend on one or two unusually good trades?") is answered no. The problem is not a fragile reliance on outliers; it's a persistently sub-50% win rate that a 2:1 reward:risk target doesn't fully compensate for once costs are included (gross breakeven win rate at 2:1 is 33.3%; the observed 34.9% clears that only barely, and net-of-cost breakeven is higher still).

The mean-R confidence interval straddles zero, so a small positive true edge cannot be strictly ruled out from this sample alone; the POINT ESTIMATE is negative and the interval leans negative, but "total PnL is negative" is not being treated as proof of "no edge" on its own (spec §12's explicit instruction) — the t-statistic and CI are reported instead of a bare pass/fail claim. 15Reproducibility (spec §17)

The full 12-symbol baseline experiment was run twice, both times against the (by-then-populated) local cache.

Confirmed, with one honestly-reported wrinkle

Two cache-hit runs of the entire experiment produced a byte-identical output file. The first attempt at this check compared the ORIGINAL run (which fetched several symbols live for the first time) against a repeat — those two differed at the ~1e-9 relative level in a few float fields, plus a cosmetic timezone-offset difference in timestamp strings. Investigating rather than dismissing it: this is the already-documented Phase 4.5 finding that CSV cache round-trips normalize timestamps to timezone-naive — comparing a live-fetch run to a cached-read run is not a fair reproducibility test. Re-run properly (cache-hit vs. cache-hit), the result is exactly reproducible, with no engine non-determinism involved.

18Final classification

C — no evidence of a durable edge

Every line of evidence points the same direction: pooled net loss at realistic costs; a gross (zero-cost) edge of roughly zero; only a third of symbols individually profitable; a mean-R confidence interval that cannot rule out zero and leans negative; consistent (not decaying) weakness across development/validation/out-of-sample; and — most tellingly — buy-and-hold beating the strategy in 9 of 12 cases, often by an order of magnitude. The strategy is not "profitable" and this report does not use that word for it. The one genuinely interesting pattern (regime concentration in strong-uptrend quarters) is real and worth investigating, but it describes a DIFFERENT, narrower, not-yet-tested strategy (a regime-gated variant) — it does not rescue the verdict on the strategy as currently specified and gated.

19What this evidence does NOT prove (spec §23)

  • This does not prove the software is broken. Every engineering guarantee from Phases 1-7A (determinism, no look-ahead, restart safety, paper/backtest parity, idempotency) held throughout this phase's much larger, more varied real-data workload. The platform works correctly; it correctly computed a losing strategy.
  • This does not prove NO trend/momentum strategy can ever work on these instruments. It evaluates one specific, frozen rule set (SMA20/50 + RSI14 + MACD + volume-slope + ATR-based 1.5x stop + 2:1 target) — not the universe of possible strategies.
  • This does not prove the regime-concentration pattern is real and exploitable. It was observed by inspecting this phase's own results; testing it properly requires a fresh validation on data/periods not used to generate the hypothesis, to avoid data-snooping contamination (spec §16). That is future work, explicitly not started here.
  • This does not extrapolate to a different time period, cost structure, or account size. All results are specific to the stated 5-year window, this cost model, and this universe.
  • A wider confidence interval that includes zero is not the same as proof of zero edge. The mean-R interval straddling zero means a small true positive edge cannot be strictly excluded from this sample — the verdict rests on the WEIGHT of multiple converging lines of evidence (cost sensitivity, benchmark, walk-forward, per-symbol breadth), not on that single interval.

20Recommendation for the next phase

Do not proceed to live trading, broker integration, or further infrastructure investment premised on this strategy having an edge — the evidence doesn't support that premise. Two legitimate, narrower next steps exist, to be pursued as SEPARATE, freshly-validated work rather than by re-running this experiment with adjustments: (1) formally specify and validate a regime-gated variant (e.g., only take signals when a broader trend filter confirms a strong-uptrend regime) against data/periods not used to generate that hypothesis here; (2) treat this platform's genuinely proven strength — a correct, deterministic, restart-safe, fully-tested research and paper-trading pipeline — as the valuable deliverable of Phases 1-8, independent of any one strategy's performance, and use it to evaluate future strategy candidates with the same rigor applied here.
